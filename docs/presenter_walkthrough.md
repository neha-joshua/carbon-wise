# CarbonWise: a plain-language tour

## What it does

CarbonWise brings together three ways to explore climate investment planning in Chennai:

1. **Analyze my data** lets a visitor upload a CSV or Excel project list, map its columns, check the entries, set a budget, and compare possible plans.
2. **Try a sample plan** demonstrates the planner using 56 generated example projects and 15 GCC zone records. These are examples, not actual city proposals or measured outcomes.
3. **Explore official evidence** lets a visitor inspect source-linked records from the supplied GCC project list, GCC's Climate Budget, and public contract-award listings.

It is a research prototype. It is not an official GCC service, a live sensor system, or a digital twin.

## A quick demo

1. Open **Try a sample plan**.
2. Set the available budget in the left panel. Adjust the three community-need priorities if useful. Their values affect only the sample plan.
3. Open **Example projects** to filter and inspect the generated entries, or **Areas** to see the 15 zone records.
4. Choose **Build sample plan**.
5. Review the budget used and the three relative sample scores. Expand **See comparisons, rationale, and fairness checks** for alternatives and details.
6. Download the report if needed. Its header and explanatory notes identify the generated data and score limitations.

For a second demo, select **Analyze my data**, load the included synthetic example, review its column mapping and checks, then set a budget and generate a plan. The included file is also generated; replace it with data you have permission to use. Missing required values are flagged instead of being silently filled.

## What the numbers mean

- **Carbon, resilience, and community scores** in the sample planner are relative model scores. They are not tonnes of emissions, measured flood protection, or proof that residents received benefits.
- **Budget used** is the selected example rows' listed cost divided by the budget entered.
- **Where the example investment goes** compares how generated project costs are distributed across zones. It does not measure resident outcomes or prove fairness.
- **Budget comparisons** rerun the model at different limits. They show how sample results change; they do not forecast city outcomes.
- The optimizer searches for feasible sample plans. It may not find every possible combination.

## What the evidence page supports

The evidence workspace links supplied project records to published GCC material and shows tender-award references. Each claim should be read with its source and status. A published estimate is still an estimate; an award is not proof of payment or completion. The app keeps actual expenditure and causal effects unknown where the reviewed sources do not establish them.

The GCC evidence is not ranked by the sample optimizer. The records do not share a validated project-level resilience measure, cost scope, and project-to-zone/beneficiary mapping needed for a fair like-for-like ranking.

## Street View Insights

In **Analyze my data**, an uploaded or captured photo can be summarized by its colour shares. Green-coloured pixels are not confirmed trees; grey pixels are not confirmed roads. The feature does not identify objects, estimate emissions, or infer flood risk. A person must confirm their own observation, and may leave it unlinked to a project area. Notes are kept for the current session; download them if they need to be saved.

## Two-minute presentation script

“CarbonWise gives users three starting points: analyze their own project data, try a clearly labelled sample plan, or explore source-linked Chennai records. The sample planner lets someone set a budget and compare plans across relative carbon, resilience, and community scores. Those scores are not physical impact measurements, and the example projects are generated rather than real municipal proposals. The evidence workspace is separate: it shows what GCC and procurement sources report, with links and unknowns alongside each record. We do not claim that a published estimate proves a realized outcome, or that a contract award proves payment or completion. To rank real city projects responsibly, the next data needed are comparable project costs, verified locations and beneficiaries, completion and payment records, and measured outcomes.”

## Safe claims for a review

- “The source reports this status, quantity, or estimate on the linked page.”
- “We reproduced the published arithmetic; that does not independently verify its assumptions or the real-world impact.”
- “This is an award record. The reviewed listing does not establish actual expenditure or completion.”
- “The sample planner demonstrates the comparison method on generated inputs; it does not recommend actual GCC funding.”
